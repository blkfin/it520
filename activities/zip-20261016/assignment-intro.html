Does ZIP help this file?

IT 520 · Friday, October 16 · Individual, asynchronous activity · About 40 minutes

A smaller file is not the whole story. In this activity, you will investigate how ZIP behaves on four different files: a repetitive log, ordinary prose, a JPEG photograph, and a tiny note. You will predict what happens, collect measurements, check exact recovery, and make a recommendation supported by your evidence.

Complete the activity in Google Colab. Submit the shared URL of your own completed Colab notebook to this Canvas assignment by Saturday, at the deadline shown in Canvas.

Get started

  1. Open the activity directly in Google Colab and sign in with your Google account.
  2. Choose File → Save a copy in Drive and put your name in the notebook title.

All four input files are included in the notebook. You do not need to install anything, upload separate data files, or connect your Google Drive to the code.

What to do

  1. Run the supplied input cell, inspect the files, and write your predictions before running the compression cell.
  2. Run the prepared ZIP experiment. Record the original and complete archive sizes. Calculate the compression ratio and percentage saved, enter your values in the Q2 cell, and rerun that cell until the arithmetic checker says PASS.
  3. Run the recovery checks and the deliberately changed-copy comparison. Record the paths and results, then explain what they establish.
  4. Complete questions Q1–Q9, citing your measurements. Explain which files you would ZIP and why. Distinguish saving bytes from saving time; this experiment does not measure transfer speed.

The code is supplied. The only code editing required is entering your Q2 calculations. Double-click a written-answer cell to replace its placeholders, then press Shift+Enter. Work through the notebook in order; do not use “Run all” before recording your predictions. The extension at the end is optional.

What to submit
